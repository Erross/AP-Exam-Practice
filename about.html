AP EXAM PRACTICE

About AP Exam Practice

This site is an unofficial, free practice tool focused on the multiple-choice portion of AP exams. It is designed to give students realistic timed Section I practice without requiring an account.

What it does

For each released course, the site builds a full-length multiple-choice practice section using original questions. The practice configuration follows the course's current Section I structure, including question count, timing, unit weighting, skills, and stimulus-set requirements where those apply.

  • Shows the practice format before the timer starts.
  • Runs the section under timed conditions.
  • Supports course-specific timing or calculator parts where configured.
  • Saves an in-progress attempt locally in this browser so a refresh does not automatically destroy the session.
  • Provides a score, per-unit breakdown, and answer review after submission.

What is available now

The home page is the source of truth for course availability. Courses shown under Available now have a released multiple-choice bank and can be practiced immediately. Courses shown under In development are not yet available for practice.

Released courses are intended to have enough original material to support repeated practice while keeping the exam draw aligned to that course's configured blueprint.

What it does not currently include

This is currently an MCQ-only practice site. It does not provide free-response questions, essays, written-response tasks, document-based questions, performance tasks, oral responses, or scoring for those parts of an AP exam.

That means this site should not be treated as a complete simulation of an AP exam when the official exam also contains written, free-response, spoken, portfolio, or performance components. The full-exam duration shown on a course card is context; the timed practice itself covers the multiple-choice section described on that card.

Original and unofficial

The questions on this site are original practice material. They are not official College Board questions, and this site is not affiliated with or endorsed by College Board. References to AP course names and exam structures are used to describe the practice target.

Accounts, privacy, and saved progress

No account is required. In-progress practice state is stored locally in your browser rather than being tied to a user profile. Clearing browser storage, switching browsers, or changing devices can remove that saved state.

How to use it

Choose a released course, review the format screen, then start the timed section when you are ready. Use the results and answer review to identify weak units and then take another practice draw later.
